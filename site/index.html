When a token dies the chart just stops, and whoever is still holding it gets no explanation. Everything needed to reconstruct what happened is public. Vane assembles it into something a person can read — who deployed it, how old that wallet was, who took supply in the launch block, and the single transaction that ended it.

Not live yet. The tooling behind it is public and working today.

Observables, never intent

The difference between a useful report and a lawsuit is one sentence, so it is worth being exact about which sentences Vane will write.

“The deployer wallet existed for 84 minutes before launch, and took 100% of supply in the launch block.” A fact. Every figure reproducible from the signatures cited.
“This is a scam.” An accusation. Vane does not make them.

What it does

  • Reconstructs the chronology — deployment, launch block, liquidity curve, the death event
  • Names the sample size next to every rate, including the ones that look bad
  • States what it cannot see on every report, with a versioned detector id
  • Publishes aggregates first — launchpad survival needs no prediction

What it won't

  • No verdicts. "Rug" is not a detector output
  • No faces. Accounts that mentioned a token are listed by when, never as participants
  • No accounts, no tracking. Nothing is stored about you
  • No confident numbers for things that cannot be measured

Built in the open, mistakes included

A tool that claims to tell you what really happened has to be willing to show when it got something wrong. Four parser bugs so far, two still open — all of which returned plausible wrong answers rather than errors. The build log says what works today and what doesn't.

Free, and intended to stay that way. There is no paywall, no account and no tracking — the analytics that would tell us whether any of this is working are the same analytics we refuse to run. Reports cost compute; if the tool is useful to you, support keeps it running.

Charging someone for an explanation of how they were rugged is insult on top of injury.

Nothing here is financial advice. Vane publishes descriptions of things that already happened on a public blockchain. It does not tell you what to buy, hold or sell, it does not predict prices, and it has no opinion on whether any token is a good idea. Do your own research, and do not treat anything on this site as a substitute for it.

Reports describe on-chain events, not people. A wallet is not a person, and Vane does not know who controls one. Nothing published here is an allegation that any individual or organisation has committed fraud, broken a law, or acted in bad faith — and statements about timing, balances and transactions should not be read as implying it. Where an account is listed as having mentioned a token, that records only that it posted and when.

The data can be wrong. It is derived from public chain data by software with known limitations, which are published alongside every report and in the findings. Every figure cites the transaction signatures it came from so you can check it yourself. If something is wrong, tell us and it will be corrected in public.
